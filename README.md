# Elements on Demand / 按需制造元素

What would it take to manufacture selected elements economically and controllably?

怎样才能以可控、经济的方式制造指定元素？

---

## 中文

### 我想提出的问题

我们已经能够通过化学方法制造种类繁多的材料，但化学反应主要改变电子状态和原子之间的连接，通常不改变元素身份。

如果希望把一种元素变成另一种元素，就需要改变原子核中的质子数。人类已经实现了部分元素转化，但距离灵活、经济、有实际产量的“按需制造元素”，仍有很大距离。

我的核心问题是：

**哪些科学与工程突破，能够使指定元素的制造从少量实现，走向具有实际用途的生产？**

### “制造”的含义

本讨论中的制造，是把原料中的原子转化为目标元素的原子，而不只是从矿物或混合物中提取已有元素。

需要明确区分：

- 元素转化：改变元素身份。
- 同位素制造：获得指定质子数和中子数的核素。
- 分离与富集：从已有混合物中提高目标组分的比例。

这些过程可以相互配合，但不是同一件事。

### 目标与范围

讨论聚焦民用材料、稳定同位素、科研与资源利用。

“按需”不意味着任何元素都能够无限量、低成本地制造。我们希望明确哪些目标受到基本物理限制，哪些目标主要受到工程与经济条件限制。

### 希望共同讨论的问题

1. 元素制造的根本限制是什么？哪些来自能量收支、核稳定性和反应概率，哪些来自现有设备能力？

2. 高能量或高能量密度是否是决定因素？在什么情况下，反应选择性、产物收集和分离成本更重要？

3. 对不同目标元素和稳定同位素，制造难度为何相差巨大？

4. 如何评价一条制造路径？是否应同时比较原料可得性、产率、纯度、能耗、设备寿命、副产物及最终成本？

5. 哪些民用目标最有可能先实现经济可行的制造？在哪些情况下，提取、回收或替代材料仍然更合理？

6. 哪些突破可能带来实质进展：更准确的反应数据、更好的理论预测、更高效的粒子源、更耐用的材料、更好的分离技术，还是其他方向？

7. 如果要制定研究路线图，应该选择什么具体目标，并用什么可测指标判断进展？

### 欢迎怎样的贡献？

- 公开研究文献与数据。
- 清楚说明假设的数量级估算。
- 具体民用案例和技术经济分析。
- 对不合理前提的批评。
- 明确指出原则上不可实现的目标。

不预设存在某种尚未证实的低能转化机制，也不把无法解释的测量结果直接视为元素转化。

希望把一个宏大的愿望，拆解成有证据、可计算、可验证的问题。

## English

### The question

Chemistry allows us to manufacture an enormous range of materials. However, chemical reactions primarily change electronic states and connections between atoms; they generally do not change elemental identity.

Converting one element into another requires changing the number of protons in the atomic nucleus. Some transformations are already possible, but flexible, economical production at useful scales remains a much broader challenge.

**What scientific and engineering advances could move the manufacture of selected elements from small-scale demonstrations to practically useful production?**

### What “manufacturing” means here

Manufacturing means converting atoms in a feedstock into atoms of a target element, rather than merely extracting an element already present in a mineral or mixture.

We distinguish:

- Element transmutation: changing elemental identity.
- Isotope production: obtaining a specified nuclide with particular proton and neutron numbers.
- Separation and enrichment: increasing the proportion of a component already present.

These processes can complement one another, but they are not interchangeable.

### Goals and scope

The discussion focuses on civilian materials, stable isotopes, scientific research, and resource utilization.

“On demand” does not imply that every element can be manufactured cheaply or in unlimited quantities. The aim is to distinguish fundamental physical limits from engineering and economic constraints.

### Questions for discussion

1. What fundamentally limits element manufacturing? Which constraints arise from energy balance, nuclear stability, and reaction probabilities, and which arise from existing equipment?

2. Is high energy or high energy density the decisive factor? When do selectivity, product recovery, and separation costs matter more?

3. Why does production difficulty vary so widely between target elements and stable isotopes?

4. How should production pathways be compared? Relevant measures may include feedstock availability, yield, purity, energy use, equipment lifetime, byproducts, and final cost.

5. Which civilian targets are most likely to become economically viable? When would extraction, recycling, or material substitution remain preferable?

6. Which advances could make a substantial difference: better reaction data, predictive models, more efficient particle sources, more durable materials, improved separation, or something else?

7. What specific target and measurable milestones would make a useful research roadmap?

### Contributions welcome

- Public research papers and datasets.
- Order-of-magnitude estimates with explicit assumptions.
- Civilian case studies and techno-economic analyses.
- Criticism of questionable premises.
- Clear explanations of fundamentally unattainable goals.

This discussion does not assume an unverified low-energy transmutation mechanism. Unexplained measurements alone should not be treated as evidence of element transmutation.

The aim is to turn a broad ambition into questions that can be supported by evidence, calculated, and tested.
